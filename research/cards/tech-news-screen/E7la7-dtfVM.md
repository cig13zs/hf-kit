## 1. Idea and promise
The title is a second-person threat: a company can look up where *you* have been. The thumbnail
was not reviewed. The video delivers the premise in its first 19 seconds, walking the viewer's own
car through the camera, the neural network and a nationwide database (seen, transcript). The
promise is named at 0:29.6-0:42.4: how the cameras work, the old legal loophole, and people
mapping the cameras back (transcript). The curiosity gap is how this is legal. The answer comes at
3:00-3:28 (transcript).

## 8. Retention mechanics: why
- "You" in the first sentence. The viewer's own drive is the example, which makes a policy
  story personal. The job is building tension.
- Show the pipeline. Camera, model, cloud, database, each as one icon frame. The job is
  clarity: a system you can picture is a system you can be angry at.
- Named abuse cases. Three short news cases at 3:32-3:54 turn "this could be abused" into
  "this was abused" (transcript). The job is delivering proof.
- A counter-hero at the end. The open-source map and its cease-and-desist reply give the story
  a win before the sponsor. The job is showing a payoff.

## 9. Outlier against control
This is the control. Both outliers cut slower (11.0-13.3 against 15.6 cuts/min) and show more text
(57-72% against 47% of frames, 10.6-21.2 against 6.7 words per frame) (measured). Hypothesis: on
this channel, the topic (a frontier-AI claim or a feud at a famous lab) and document receipts
separate the outliers, not cut pace. A second hypothesis: this video explains a system with
diagrams and b-roll, while the outliers report a fight with named people, and the channel's
audience rewards the fight. See `niches/tech-news-screen.md`.
