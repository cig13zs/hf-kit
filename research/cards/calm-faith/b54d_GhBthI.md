## 8. Retention mechanics: why
- **Forty seconds with no cut, while the problem is stated.** The camera travels from the
  leader to the crowd to the empty desert, so the viewer feels the "long way" instead of
  being told it. This is the job of clarity and of tension. It works because the words are
  dense (152 wpm) and the move keeps travelling; a static 40-second frame would not (inferred).
- **The question asked aloud by a second voice.** "But why?" turns a Bible recap into an open
  loop within 16 seconds. This is the job of tension.
- **A shot floor of 5 seconds.** No image leaves before it is read. This is clarity, and it
  sets a rhythm the viewer can relax into (inferred). It is what "calm" means in this edit:
  predictable, not slow.
- **Colour as a map.** Orange means distance from God, green means life, the swirl means
  presence. The viewer can follow the argument with the sound off (inferred). This is clarity.
- **The cut rate rises at the climax.** About 10 a minute in 4:08-4:49 against 5 elsewhere.
  Energy is spent once, where the story turns from failure to success. This resets
  attention and shows the payoff.
- **A breath before the application.** The 5 LU dip and the white frame at 5:02 separate
  "what God did" from "where you are now". This is the job of setting the rhythm.
- What the slow edit knows: long shots are safe when narration density stays at about 150
  wpm and every cut delivers the noun being spoken.
